Short summary

Chapter 1 introduces deep learning as a set of techniques for learning representations of data using multi-layered models (neural networks). The chapter motivates the field by showing how learned hierarchical representations can replace hand-designed features across domains such as vision, speech, and language. It sketches the brief history of neural networks, outlines why recent advances (data availability, compute, algorithmic improvements) have made deep learning effective, and frames key tasks: supervised learning, unsupervised learning, and reinforcement learning. The chapter also sets up major themes that recur throughout the book: representation learning, optimization difficulties in deep models, generalization behavior, and the interplay between probabilistic modelling and deep architectures.
